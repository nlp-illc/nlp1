# Description

As the final assignment in the course, you will write a blog post of maximum 1500 words (+- 10%), in which you present and **discuss an interesting aspect of your research** from the past 6 weeks. 
This blog post is not meant to be a report with all details of the different things you tried throughout the course, and is also not meant to step-by-step tutorial to instruct others in how to carry out your experiments (though this is a popular category of blog posts), but rather **focus on your highlights and the relevance but also challenges of the dataset you chose**. 
You are encouraged to include images (these can be figures with results, but also images or animations that illustrate your set-up), use less formal writing than you would find in an academic paper, and are not restricted to pre-specified sections like “Introduction”, “Related work” and “Methodology”. 
Still, it is a scientific blog post, in which you not only cite the creators of the dataset and other artefacts you use, but also connect your findings to the literature. Here are some do’s and don’ts for writing blog posts (adapted from [Maastricht University's Blogger Dos and Donts](https://www.maastrichtuniversity.nl/blogger-dos-and-donts)):


* Do: Be direct. Get to the point quickly.
* Do: Be professional, but also be friendly.
* Do: Use examples and anecdotes to clarify your points (Note that your statements should also be supported by evidence from your experiments, examples and anecdotes are not enough to make a point)
* Do: Find a catchy title.
* Do: Proofread and double check. Read your post backwards and make sure to spell check.
* Do: Use short sentences. Simplify complex words. Divide long texts into paragraphs with headers.
* Do: Use images. Pictures and graphics will make your blog more attractive.


* Don't: Ramble with run-on sentences that just keep on dragging or lengthy texts.
* Don't: Try to cover too many topics at once.
* Don't: Use profanity or unprofessional language.
* Don't: Use other people’s stuff without permission and credit.
* Don't: Combine too many style effects like **bold**, _italic_, or <ins>underline</ins>. Things become hard to read.


Your target audience for the blog post is an NLP 1 peer, who now knows what a tokenizer is and how word embeddings work, but does not know the specifics of your dataset, of the tokenizer and word embeddings you might have used, or what experiments you conducted. 
Your own TA will be grading your blog post, so for individual feedback please reach out to them during the dedicated seminar sessions.

In the blog post, you should **disclose any use of AI coding assistants / agents** (this does not count towards the 1500 word word count).

You will write the blog post in the same groups as you did the earlier assignments and submit it on Canvas. 

# Write your post

1. Clone or fork our course's [repository](https://github.com/nlp-illc/nlp1).
2. Write your blog post under `_posts/2026/year-month-day-groupN.md` substituting your group number for `groupN`. 
3. If you need any assets, such as images, you can keep them under `/assets/blog/2026/groupN/`

# Publish 

We will grade your post and provide feedback. Once we send you feedback, you may make minor changes to incorporate it. 
Then, if you wish, you can open a pull request for us to publish your post. 

The decision to make the post publicly visible **will not affect your grade** in the assignment.
