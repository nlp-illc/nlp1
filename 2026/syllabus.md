# Lectures


* Week 1: How Language Works
    * HC1a: [Course organisation and what NLP is](./slides/HC1a-Introduction.pdf) and [Language](./slides/HC1a-Language.pdf)
    * HC1b: [Mental grammar and mental dictionary](./slides/HC1a-How-Language-Works.pdf)
    * Self-study: 
        * After HC1b: [Chapter 2 (Words and Tokens)](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/2.pdf)
        * Before HC2a: Tutorial 1 (Digital Text and Text Classification)
* Week 2: The Building Blocks of NLU and NLG 
    * HC2a: [Text classification (generalised-linear and non-linear models)](./slides/HC2a-Text-Classification.pdf)
    * HC2b: Text generation (Markov and autoregressive models)
    * Self-study
        * Before or after HC2a: [Chapter 4](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/4.pdf) (skip 4.11; treat 4.15 as optional)
        * Before or after HC2b: [Chapter 14 (sections 14.1 and 14.2 only)](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/14.pdf)
* Week 3: Meaning
    * HC3a: Distributional semantics
    * HC3b: Compositionality
* Week 4: Discourse
    * HC4a: Document representations and coreference
* Week 5: The Building Blocks of Modern NLP
    * HC5a: Sequence modelling
    * HC5b: Transfer learning
* Week 6: Large Language Models
    * HC6a: Training, adaptation and applications of LLMs
    * HC6b: LLM post-training, safety and alignment
* Week 7: Looking Inside and Looking Ahead
    * HC7a: Interpretability
    * HC7b: Broader Impact and conclusion
* Week 8: Exam week
