# Lectures


* Week 1: How Language Works
    * HC1a: [Course organisation and what NLP is](./slides/HC1a-Introduction.pdf) and [Language](./slides/HC1a-Language.pdf)
    * HC1b: [Mental grammar and mental dictionary](./slides/HC1a-How-Language-Works.pdf)
    * Self-study: 
        * After HC1b: [Chapter 2 (Words and Tokens)](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/2.pdf)
        * Before HC2a: Tutorial 1 (Digital Text and Text Classification)
* Week 2: The Building Blocks of NLU and NLG 
    * HC2a: [Text classification (generalised-linear and non-linear models)](./slides/HC2a-Text-Classification.pdf)
    * HC2b: [Text generation (Markov and autoregressive models)](./slides/HC2b-Text-Generation.pdf)
    * Self-study
        * Before or after HC2a: [Chapter 4](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/4.pdf) (skip 4.11; treat 4.15 as optional)
        * Before or after HC2b: [Chapter 14 (sections 14.1 and 14.2 only)](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/14.pdf)
        * After HC2b: [Chapter 3](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/3.pdf) (sections 3.2, 3.3 and 3.4; 3.1 is optional but useful as NGram LMs are a nice way to build intuition about more complex LMs; you can safely skip everything from and including section 3.6)
* Week 3: Meaning
    * HC3a: [Distributional semantics](./slides/HC3a-Distributional-Semantics.pdf)
    * HC3b: [Compositional semantics](./slides/HC3b-Compositional-Semantics.pdf)
    * Self-study:
        * Before or after HC3a: [Chapter 5](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/5.pdf) (sections 5.2-5.5 and 5.7)
        * After HC3b: [Chapter 6](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/6.pdf) (sections 6.3--6.6) and [Chapter 14](http://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/14) (sections 14.3.2, 14.4 and 14.5)
* Week 4: Discourse
    * HC4a: [Document representations and coreference](./slides/HC4a-Discourse-Processing.pdf)
    * Self-study:
        * Before or after HC4a: [Chapter 25](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/25.pdf) (section 25.1) and [Chapter 24](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/25.pdf) (sections 24.1-24.3, 24.5-24.6)
* Week 5: The Building Blocks of Modern NLP
    * HC5a: [Sequence modelling](./slides/HC5a-Sequence-modelling.pdf)
    * HC5b: [Transformers and Transfer learning](./slides/HC5b-Transformers-Transfer-Learning.pdf)
    * Self-study:
        * Before or after HC5a: [Chapter 13](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/13.pdf) (sections 13.2, 13.3, 13.4, 13.6; this chapter is about MT but the approaches sketched, including for evalution, are common to many such 'sequence-to-sequence' problems; in fact, much of this technology was driven by MT research)
        * Before or after HC5b: For the Transformer architecture in general, [Chapter 7](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/7.pdf). For masked language models, [Chapter 9](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/9.pdf) (sections 9.1 to 9.3). For instruction tuning and reinforcement learning from human feedback, [Chapter 8](https://web.stanford.edu/~jurafsky/slp3/8.pdf) (sections 8.1 to 8.3).
* Week 6: Large Language Models
    * HC6a: Training, adaptation and applications of LLMs
    * HC6b: LLM post-training, safety and alignment
* Week 7: Looking Inside and Looking Ahead
    * HC7a: Interpretability
    * HC7b: Broader Impact and conclusion
* Week 8: Exam week
